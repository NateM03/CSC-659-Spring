## 3. My own opinion on AI regulation:

### a) Pros and cons of AI regulations as I see them

One of the biggest pros of AI regulation is that it can put everyone on the same page about how AI systems work, where information comes from, and what standards should be followed. Without regulation, people may use AI in very different ways without knowing whether the output is reliable, biased, or original. Some level of regulation can improve transparency and accountability by making it clearer when AI is being used, what information or sources are behind it, and who is responsible when something goes wrong. I think this is important because AI is becoming more common in education, business, and everyday decision-making, so there should be shared rules instead of confusion.

At the same time, one of the biggest cons of AI regulation is that it could reduce creativity, originality, and independent thinking if it is not designed well. If people become too dependent on AI, they may stop developing their own ideas and begin relying on AI input for everything. This could make work feel less original and could weaken real learning, problem-solving, and creativity. In my view, the problem is not only the technology itself, but also the possibility that people may begin using it as a replacement for thinking rather than as a tool to support thinking.

### b) My position on whether AI should be regulated

My position is that AI should be partially regulated. I do not think every use of AI needs heavy restrictions, because AI can be a normal and useful tool when people use it responsibly. However, I also do not think AI should be left completely unregulated. A balanced approach makes more sense to me. AI should be allowed as a tool to help people learn, research, and improve their understanding, but it should not replace human judgment, effort, or originality. Because of that, I support targeted regulation rather than blanket regulation.

### c) Feasibility and how I would implement it

If I were to regulate AI, I would focus on rules that keep it useful without allowing it to do all the work for people. First, I would support rules that make AI useful for learning, reading, studying, and practicing, since those uses can help people become more informed. Second, I would require more transparency about when AI is being used and where its information comes from, so users are not misled into thinking everything is fully original or fully human-created. Third, I would limit situations where AI can generate complete final outputs with little or no human effort, especially in education or other areas where independent thinking matters. In those cases, AI should help start the process by giving basic guidance, explanations, or structure, but the final work should still depend on the person. I think this kind of regulation is feasible because it does not ban AI entirely. Instead, it sets reasonable boundaries so AI remains a support tool rather than becoming a replacement for human learning and originality.
